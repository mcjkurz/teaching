# 種族暴力 · CHI 4335 第 12 週

Open `index.html` in a browser. Keys: ← → step, 1–5 sections, G go to slide, L laser pointer, F fullscreen.

Readings: Grace Elizabeth Hale, “Deadly Amusements: Spectacle Lynchings and the Contradictions of Segregation as Culture,” in *Making Whiteness* (1998), pp. 199–239 (page numbers on the slides follow the print edition); Frantz Fanon, *Black Skin, White Masks* (Philcox translation, 2008), ch. 1 and ch. 5 “The Lived Experience of the Black Man” (cited by chapter only); Susan Sontag, *Regarding the Pain of Others* (Picador, 2004), pp. 18 and 91–92, with the Chinese translation used in Week 2. Hale and Fanon are quoted in English with provisional Chinese translations (暫譯).

Structure (44 slides, each with one quotation or a few bullet points and one image):

1. History (歷史背景): the Atlantic slave trade (triangle map, the Brookes slave ship); the ban on teaching slaves to read (Douglass, *Narrative*, 1845, ch. 6); *Uncle Tom's Cabin* (1852; 林紓 1901; Baldwin's 1949 critique); Civil War and Reconstruction; Hale ch. 1 on progress and segregation (Ida B. Wells; the segregated cinema entrance in Belzoni, Mississippi, 1939); Jim Crow (T. D. Rice); segregation as culture (including schools); Anna Julia Cooper; blackface (Billy Van poster, set up as the reverse of Fanon's title), followed by *The Birth of a Nation* (1915; clips at 2:15:00–2:20:00 and 2:27:00–2:30:00), which carries blackface from the stage to the screen; the Great Migration (map `img/migration_map.svg`, drawn over the public-domain Wikimedia "Blank US Map (states only)"); the Harlem Renaissance (footnote on the pronunciation of Du Bois); Black writers and poets (Hughes, Hurston, McKay, Cullen, Wright); Hughes, “I, Too” (1926), followed by Whitman, “I Hear America Singing” (1860), the poem it answers; the Second World War and returning soldiers (Tuskegee Airmen, Isaac Woodard).
2. Hale (私刑作為景觀): modern spectacle (Kodak advertisement), spectacle versus narrative, Henry Smith 1893, the standardized script as a numbered sequence, newspapers, souvenirs (the finale of the script), whiteness by class and gender.
3. Looking and onlookers (觀看與看客, following Sontag): Hale on participation and looking (Marion 1930), spectatorship as a modern experience, Sontag on the lynching photographs (Omaha 1919), Du Bois and the knuckles in the grocery window (1899), Lu Xun's Sendai lantern slide (with a late-Qing beheading photograph taken from the Week 2 deck), the two turns compared (Lu Xun and Du Bois), the dilemma (Mamie Till), Sontag's questions about exhibiting the photographs (Emmett Till).
4. Fanon: biography, white masks and language (ch. 1), “an object among other objects”, “Look! A Negro!” (compared with Countee Cullen), three schemas, Y a bon Banania, overdetermination and the microtome, waiting for himself at the cinema.
5. Tutorial: James Baldwin, 〈去見那個男人〉 (1965), summarized without quotation.
6. Last slide: announcement for the talk by Wayne C. F. Yeung at Lingnan on 24 November 2026 (poster `img/yeung_talk_1124.jpg`, unrelated to the course; remove after the event).

When opened from a local file, the clip buttons open YouTube in a new tab. Names of modern scholars are written in Latin script.

Historical photographs (Paris, Texas 1893; Waco 1916; Duluth 1920; Omaha 1919; Marion 1930) come from the Library of Congress and Wikimedia Commons and are captioned with the victims' names.
