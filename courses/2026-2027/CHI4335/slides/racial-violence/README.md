# 種族暴力 · CHI 4335 第 12 週

Open `index.html` in a browser. Keys: ← → step, 1–5 sections, G go to slide, L laser pointer, F fullscreen.

Readings: Grace Elizabeth Hale, “Deadly Amusements: Spectacle Lynchings and the Contradictions of Segregation as Culture,” in *Making Whiteness* (1998), pp. 199–239 (page numbers on the slides follow the print edition); Frantz Fanon, “The Lived Experience of the Black Man,” in *Black Skin, White Masks* (Philcox translation, 2008), ch. 5 (cited by chapter only). Both are quoted in English with provisional Chinese translations (暫譯). Structure (29 slides, each built around one longer quotation or a few bullet points): historical background (Civil War map, Reconstruction, post-war friction and segregation as a response to black success from Hale ch. 1, Jim Crow, lynching statistics, segregation as culture, Anna Julia Cooper at the segregated station); Hale's arguments (modern spectacle, spectacle versus narrative from the Introduction, Henry Smith 1893, the standardized script, newspapers, whiteness by class, gender and nation with *The Birth of a Nation* clips at 2:15:00–2:20:00 and 2:27:00–2:30:00, souvenirs, Du Bois); looking and onlookers (participation, Walter White, Sontag, Lu Xun's Sendai lantern slide, the counter-spectacle); Fanon (the gaze, three schemas, overdetermination). When opened from a local file, the clip buttons open YouTube in a new tab. Names of modern scholars are written in Latin script. Tutorial: James Baldwin, 〈去見那個男人〉 (1965), summarized without quotation.

Historical photographs (Paris, Texas 1893; Waco 1916; Duluth 1920; Marion 1930) come from the Library of Congress and Wikimedia Commons (public domain) and are captioned with the victims' names; slide 2 is a content note. Text versions of the readings are in `misc/racial-violence/txt/`. All images are in `img/`.
